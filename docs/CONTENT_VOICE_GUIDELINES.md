# Content Voice Guidelines - Dale Rogers Portfolio

## Purpose

This document establishes the authoritative voice and tone standards for all content across Dale Rogers' portfolio. Consistent voice builds trust, reinforces expertise, and creates a memorable brand presence.

---

## Voice vs. Tone

**Voice** = WHO you are (consistent across all content)
**Tone** = HOW you adjust for context (varies by situation)

**Dale's Voice**: Experienced, practical, human-centered, confident, approachable

---

## Core Voice Principles

### 1. First-Person Professional

**Use**: "I help organisations..." NOT "Dale helps organisations..."

**Rationale**: Creates personal connection while maintaining authority. You're speaking directly to the reader, not writing about yourself in third person.

**Examples**:

✅ **Correct**:

> I'm a service designer who helps organisations transform how they deliver value.

❌ **Incorrect**:

> Dale Rogers is a service designer who helps organisations transform how they deliver value.

✅ **Correct**:

> Over my decades in service design, I've learned that the best solutions emerge from deep collaboration.

❌ **Incorrect**:

> With decades of experience, Dale Rogers has learned that the best solutions emerge from deep collaboration.

**Exception**: Third-person is acceptable in:

- Meta descriptions for SEO
- Biography sections specifically labeled as "About Dale"
- Client testimonials (they refer to you)

---

### 2. Confident Without Arrogance

**Use**: State expertise directly, let work speak for itself

**Rationale**: Leaders don't apologize for their expertise, but they don't inflate it either.

**Examples**:

✅ **Correct**:

> I've spent decades designing services for government and enterprise organisations.

❌ **Too Tentative**:

> I've had the opportunity to work with some government organisations.

❌ **Too Arrogant**:

> As one of Australia's leading service designers, I've revolutionized...

✅ **Correct**:

> This approach consistently delivers measurable improvements in user satisfaction and operational efficiency.

❌ **Too Hesitant**:

> This approach sometimes seems to help with user satisfaction.

---

### 3. Practical Over Academic

**Use**: Real-world examples and plain language

**Rationale**: Service design is about application, not theory. Demonstrate expertise through clarity, not jargon.

**Examples**:

✅ **Correct**:

> Service design maps the entire journey—from first touchpoint to final outcome—identifying friction points and opportunities for improvement.

❌ **Too Academic**:

> Service design employs ethnographic methodologies and systems thinking paradigms to conceptualize touchpoint orchestration within complex adaptive systems.

✅ **Correct**:

> I start with people. What do they need? What frustrates them? What would make their experience better?

❌ **Too Theoretical**:

> Utilizing human-centered design frameworks, I engage in empathetic inquiry to surface latent user needs and pain points.

**Guideline**: If you'd say it in a client meeting, use it. If it sounds like a textbook, rewrite it.

---

### 4. Australian English Consistency

**Use**: Australian spelling, formatting, and idioms

**Rationale**: Authentic regional identity, clear audience targeting.

**Spelling Rules**:

- -ise not -ize (organise, realise, specialise)
- -our not -or (colour, behaviour, flavour)
- -re not -er (centre, theatre)
- -ogue not -og (catalogue, dialogue)
- -ll- in past tense (travelled, modelled)
- -ence not -ense (defence, licence as noun)

**Date Format**: DD/MM/YYYY (23/03/2024)
**Time Format**: 24-hour (14:30, not 2:30 PM)
**Currency**: $AUD or Australian dollars

**Examples**:

✅ **Correct**:

> I specialise in behaviour change, working with organisations to realise their strategic goals.

❌ **Incorrect**:

> I specialize in behavior change, working with organizations to realize their strategic goals.

---

### 5. Active Voice, Direct Address

**Use**: Action-oriented language that puts reader in the story

**Rationale**: Creates engagement and clarity. Service design is about doing, not describing.

**Examples**:

✅ **Correct**:

> We'll map your customer journey, identify pain points, and design solutions that work.

❌ **Passive**:

> The customer journey will be mapped, pain points will be identified, and solutions will be designed.

✅ **Correct**:

> Your users experience friction at three key points in the process.

❌ **Passive**:

> Friction is experienced by users at three key points in the process.

## Tone Variations by Content Type

### Homepage / Hero

**Tone**: Confident, Clear, Value-Focused

**Template**:

> [What I Do] for [Target Outcome]
>
> [Short value proposition that speaks to reader's need]

**Example**:

> Service Design for Strategic Transformation
>
> I help organisations transform how they deliver value, bridging human needs with strategic goals through decades of service design expertise.

---

### Case Studies

**Tone**: Results-Oriented, Story-Driven, Evidence-Based

**Structure**:

1. **Challenge** (what the client faced)
2. **Approach** (how I tackled it)
3. **Outcomes** (what we achieved together)
4. **Insights** (what I learned)

**Voice Guidelines**:

- Lead with impact, not process
- Use "we" when describing collaborative work
- Use "I" when describing your specific contribution
- Quantify outcomes where possible
- Include client perspective when available

**Examples**:

✅ **Strong Opening**:

> The Department faced a critical challenge: their fit-and-proper person assessment process took 8+ weeks, creating bottlenecks and frustration.

❌ **Weak Opening**:

> This project involved working with a government department to improve their processes.

✅ **Results Statement**:

> We reduced assessment time by 60%, from 8 weeks to 3 weeks, while improving quality and consistency.

❌ **Vague Results**:

> The new process was much faster and worked better.

---

### Articles / Thought Leadership

**Tone**: Insightful, Conversational, Authoritative

**Voice Guidelines**:

- Open with a clear perspective or provocative question
- Use personal anecdotes to illustrate points
- Balance theory with practice
- End with actionable takeaways

**Opening Patterns**:

✅ **Strong**:

> Culture isn't something that happens to your organisation—it's something you design, intentionally and continuously.

✅ **Strong**:

> I've watched countless digital transformation projects fail. Not because of technology, but because they ignored the people using it.

❌ **Weak**:

> In this article, I will discuss the importance of culture in organisations.

---

### Services / Offerings

**Tone**: Clear, Benefit-Focused, Actionable

**Structure**:

1. **What It Is** (clear definition)
2. **When You Need It** (problem/opportunity)
3. **How It Works** (process overview)
4. **What You Get** (deliverables/outcomes)

**Voice Guidelines**:

- Address reader as "you"
- Focus on benefits, not features
- Use concrete examples
- Include clear next step

**Example**:

✅ **Correct**:

> **Service Blueprinting**
>
> Your organisation delivers services across multiple channels—digital, physical, phone. But do you understand how they all connect? Service blueprinting maps every step, showing what customers experience and what happens behind the scenes.
>
> **You need this when**:
>
> - Launching new services across channels
> - Improving existing service delivery
> - Scaling operations consistently
>
> **How it works**:
> We'll spend 2-4 weeks mapping your service end-to-end, identifying dependencies, pain points, and opportunities. You'll get a comprehensive blueprint that your team can use for planning, training, and improvement.

---

### About / Biography

**Tone**: Personal, Authentic, Credible

**Voice Guidelines**:

- Start with present expertise, then journey
- Include personal elements that build connection
- Show values through stories, not lists
- End with invitation to connect

**Structure**:

1. Current position and expertise
2. Journey that shaped perspective
3. Values and approach
4. Personal touch (location, interests)
5. Call to connect

**Example**:

✅ **Strong Approach**:

> I'm a service designer who helps organisations create meaningful connections with their audiences through strategic design thinking.
>
> My path to service design wasn't linear. I started as an electrician in mining and manufacturing, where I learned that well-designed systems work with people, not against them. That insight—that human needs should drive design—has shaped my entire career.
>
> Today, I work with government and enterprise organisations across Australia, helping them transform how they deliver value. I believe in evidence-based methodologies, collaborative approaches, and solutions that make a real difference in people's lives.
>
> Based in Canberra on Ngunnawal Country, I'm always interested in connecting with organisations ready to rethink how they serve their communities.

## Writing Style Guidelines

### Paragraph Structure

- **Maximum 3-4 sentences** per paragraph
- **Lead with topic sentence** that states the key point
- **Support with evidence or example**
- **Connect to next paragraph** with transitional phrase

### Sentence Length

- **Vary for rhythm**: Mix short impact sentences with longer explanatory ones
- **Average 15-20 words** per sentence
- **Complex ideas**: Break into multiple simple sentences

### Lists and Bullets

- Use for scannable information
- Keep bullet points parallel in structure
- Maximum 7 items per list
- Introduce lists with clear context

### Headings

- **Clear hierarchy**: H1 → H2 → H3 (rarely deeper)
- **Action-oriented or question-based** when appropriate
- **Sentence case**, not Title Case
- **Front-load keywords** for scannability

## Vocabulary Guidelines

### Preferred Terms

| Use This       | Not This    | Rationale                                         |
| -------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Organisations  | Companies   | More inclusive (includes government, non-profits) |
| Users / People | End users   | More human, less clinical                         |
| We / I / You   | One, they   | More direct and personal                          |
| Challenge      | Problem     | More constructive framing                         |
| Outcome        | Deliverable | Focus on impact, not output                       |
| Improve        | Optimise    | Clearer intent                                    |
| Create         | Develop     | More active                                       |
| Help           | Assist      | More direct                                       |

### Words to Avoid

❌ **Avoid**: Leverage, utilize, synergy, paradigm, holistic (unless specifically appropriate)
✅ **Use**: Use, work, connection, approach, complete

❌ **Avoid**: Next-gen, cutting-edge, revolutionary, game-changing
✅ **Use**: Modern, effective, new, better

❌ **Avoid**: Very, really, actually, just, simply (weak intensifiers)
✅ **Use**: Specific metrics or remove entirely

### Technical Terms

**When to use jargon**:

- ✅ Established service design terms (journey map, touchpoint, blueprint)
- ✅ When writing for design practitioners
- ✅ When it's the clearest term available

**When to avoid jargon**:

- ❌ When plain language works as well
- ❌ When writing for general business audience
- ❌ When it obscures rather than clarifies

**Guideline**: Introduce technical terms with brief definitions on first use.

## Quality Checklist

Before publishing any content, verify:

- [ ] **First person** (I/we), not third person
- [ ] **Active voice** dominates (80%+ of sentences)
- [ ] **Australian English** spelling throughout
- [ ] **Specific examples** support claims
- [ ] **Clear value proposition** for reader
- [ ] **Scannable structure** (headings, bullets, short paragraphs)
- [ ] **Call to action** where appropriate
- [ ] **Consistent tone** for content type
- [ ] **No unnecessary jargon** or buzzwords
- [ ] **Authentic voice** (sounds like a real person)

## Voice Consistency Across Channels

### Website Content

- Homepage: Confident, value-focused
- Services: Clear, benefit-oriented
- Case Studies: Results-driven
- Articles: Insightful, conversational
- About: Personal, authentic

### LinkedIn / Professional Networks

- **Shorter paragraphs** (2-3 sentences)
- **More conversational** tone
- **Industry-specific examples**
- **Engagement-focused** (questions, polls)

### Client Proposals

- **More formal** while maintaining warmth
- **Heavy on evidence** and examples
- **Clear process** and timeline
- **Risk mitigation** and contingencies

### Presentations

- **Simpler language** (spoken, not written)
- **Story-driven** structure
- **Visual-first** (slides support, not duplicate)
- **Conversational** delivery

## Implementation Guide

### For New Content

1. **Draft freely** - get ideas down
2. **Review against voice principles** - adjust tone
3. **Check Australian English** - spelling/format
4. **Read aloud** - catch awkward phrasing
5. **Check accessibility** - reading level, structure
6. **Final checklist** - use quality checklist above

### For Existing Content Audit

1. **Identify voice inconsistencies** - mark problem areas
2. **Prioritize high-traffic pages** - homepage, services, top case studies
3. **Rewrite systematically** - apply guidelines
4. **Cross-check for coherence** - ensure flow between sections
5. **Test with readers** - does it sound like one person?

## FAQs

**Q: Can I use contractions (I'm, you'll, we've)?**
A: Yes, in most contexts. They create a more conversational, approachable tone. Avoid in very formal contexts (proposals, legal).

**Q: How formal should case studies be?**
A: Professional but readable. Use "we" for collaboration, "I" for your contribution. Focus on story and results, not jargon.

**Q: Should I mention awards or recognition?**
A: Yes, but let them support the story rather than lead it. "We won [award] for this project" not "Award-winning service designer..."

**Q: How do I balance confidence with humility?**
A: State expertise directly ("I've designed services for...") but attribute success to collaboration ("Working with the team, we..."). Share learnings honestly.

**Q: What if a client wants third-person bio?**
A: Provide it, but note you prefer first-person for your own channels. Most clients accept this.
